## New

- A Sensors window, opened with All sensors at the top of the control panel. It starts on an overview of every part of your PC, and each part lists every reading it reports, with the lowest, the highest and a graph of the last minute. Turn on a reading's switch there to put it on your overlay or in your games.
- Five advanced readings, added from the Sensors window: busiest core, a bar for each core, GPU hot spot, GPU memory clock and GPU voltage.
- A straight layout for the overlay: every reading on one line, as the in-game overlay can. Choose Tiles, List or Straight under Overlay look, which replaces the Compact HUD switch.
- In a corner or against the edge of the screen, the overlay now joins the edge, as the in-game overlay does. Drag it near an edge and it moves onto the edge and takes that shape straight away.
- The Battery reading shows whether your laptop is charging, plugged in or running on its battery, with a small sign beside its name.
- A Size slider for the overlay under Appearance. Before, the overlay could only be resized by dragging its corner in Free Drag mode.

## Fixed

- The overlay could fall behind other windows and stay there, even after clicking it or the window over it. It now stays on top.
- After a laptop's battery ran flat, the Battery reading could stay at a low figure while the laptop charged, until it was turned off and on again. It now shows the same percentage as Windows.
- The control panel kept using some of your processor the whole time it was open, even with nothing changing. It now uses next to none.
- The frame rate went blank whenever one of Pulse's own windows was in front, such as the control panel. It now keeps showing the frame rate of the game behind it.

## Improved

- Pulse uses less memory, about 130 to 190 MB less on our test laptop, and its windows no longer use your graphics card at all.
- After you turn Pulse on for a game that is already running, the in-game overlay appears as soon as you go back to the game, instead of ten seconds later.
- The position sliders now go from 0 to 100 %: 50 % is the middle of the screen, 0 and 100 % put the overlay against the edges.
- Background at 100 % is now fully solid in every look, and at 0 % the overlay can still be dragged by its empty space in Free Drag.
- On laptops with two graphics, Automatic now shows your built-in graphics while your graphics card sleeps, instead of "asleep", and goes back to the card when it wakes. A graphics choice you made yourself never changes.
- Built-in graphics now show all the memory they use, their own and what they borrow from your system memory, the same as Task Manager's GPU memory. The tile is called GPU Memory.
- On built-in graphics that report no temperature of their own, such as Intel graphics, GPU Die Temp now shows the processor's temperature, because both are one chip.
